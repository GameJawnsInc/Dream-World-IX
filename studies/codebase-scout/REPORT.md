## What this codebase actually is

367 Python modules, ~238k lines under `ff9mapkit/ff9mapkit/`, plus 98 dev-loop scripts in `tools/`, a 13-module 6,178-line Blender add-on, a 2,920-line `docsite/` static-site generator, and 348 test modules spread over **four** trees (`ff9mapkit/tests/` 306, `ff9mapkit/ff9mapkit/tests/` 32, `ff9mapkit/blender/tests/` 10, `docsite/tests/` 5 — the last of which nothing collects). Raw size is not the problem here: the biggest modules (`workspace/shell.py` 11,630 · `cli.py` 10,243 with 138 verbs · `build.py` 10,177 · `world/coastmorph.py` 7,366 · `summons/repaint.py` 6,982) are mostly wide rather than deep, and only one of them, `build.py`, shows up below as a structural finding. The two size facts that *are* load-bearing appear in their own findings: `_animdb_all.py` is 14,125 generated lines with no regeneration gate, and `content/behavior.py` is 4,141 lines the offline validator never compiles.

**Verdict.** The byte-emission layer is in better shape than its size suggests — it is grounded verbatim against shipping data, chokepointed (`build.py:1277-1282` names itself "THE chokepoint every lane funnels through"), and defended by real oracles (`test_content.py:30-39`'s SHA manifest, `eblint`'s 676-field soundness proof, `test_object_graft.py:29-32`'s identity assertion). Two adversarial reviewers killed 25 of 44 candidate findings, and they killed most of them by *citing this repo's own written reasoning* — `studies/deploy-field-promotion/PLAN.md` had already analysed and deferred a proposed refactor; `test_world_frames.py:1-11` had already refused a proposed migration by name; `tests/conftest.py` docstrings had already recorded why a proposed fixture consolidation is unsafe. That is a healthy sign and it is the reason this report recommends almost no new abstraction.

The real structural pressure is narrower and more specific than "big files": **for nearly every engine rule, config seam and safety guard in this kit, exactly one correct owner exists, and two to eight hand-rolled consumers bypass it.** `config.resolve_mod_folder` implements the documented pin precedence and has one caller. `deploystack.check_model_id_collisions` guards cross-folder GEO ids and is invoked from three deploy paths but never from `summons/`. `fsutil.exclusive_fd_lock` guards every mod-folder registration write except `summons/`'s. `eb.disasm.jump_target` states the JMP_IFNOT ABI and three modules re-derive it — one of them wrongly, with the wrong law frozen in a green test. `build.lint_all` computes six slots and the deploy renderer prints five. This is CLAUDE.md §7's own law ("a rule not enforced at the call site is not enforced") applied to the codebase rather than by it, and it is why coverage here is a property of *who remembered to call something* rather than of the code.

The repo has already demonstrated the cheap answer to this shape once and shipped it, and the answer is not always "delete the copies." `ff9mapkit/blender/ff9mapkit_blender/vendor/` holds checked-in copies of five `scene/` modules — `bgi.py` 997, `paint.py` 520, `cam.py` 455, `guide.py` 312, `bgx.py` 247, **2,531 lines, zero differing bytes** — deliberately, so the add-on is self-contained inside Blender. `build_addon.py:48 sync_vendor()` regenerates them from the canonical source and `ff9mapkit/blender/tests/test_vendor_sync.py` fails the suite the moment one drifts. *Keep the copy, gate the copy* is a live, working pattern in this tree, and every duplication finding below is measured against it.

Almost every fix recommended here is a deletion, a re-point, or a gate over an existing copy — not a new layer.

## The big picture

### 1. Where a deploy lands — the most destructive input, computed eight ways

**What is wrong at the design level.** Every destructive verb answers one question — which mod folder, which id — and the answer is derived independently at eight sites with five different roots, three failure contracts and one inverted precedence. The eight readers: `config.py:258` `_read_deploy_config` (CWD walk, stops at `.git`) · `cli.py:1767` `_deploy_cfg` (`__file__.parents[2]`) · `editor/jobs.py:118` (injected `repo_root`) · `workspace/floorplandoc.py:1916` (`self.kit.parent`) · `tools/{deploy_field,deploy_campaign,deploy_battle,mognet_donor_patch}.py` (`tools/..`). A ninth reader bypasses the question entirely: `cli._cmd_world_ledger:5477` resolves `find_game_path(args.game) / args.mod_folder` directly, one of **68** raw `args.mod_folder` uses in `cli.py`. Failure handling splits `sys.exit(2)` / warn-to-stderr / silent. Crucially, this is *not* one root cause: several roots are load-bearing (`deploy_field.py:32-37` documents its `__file__` root as deliberate, because the wheel ships no `tools/`), and the defaults differ on purpose (`FF9CustomMap-ow` for chains at `cli.py:2191`, a dedicated `FF9CustomMap-<field>` for `deploy` at `deploy.py:591`).

**The file has three keys, and the two most expensive ones have no owner at all.** `.ff9deploy.toml` carries `mod_folder`, `id` and `text_block`.

- `id` is read by `tools/deploy_field.py:57` (`_def_id = int(_cfg.get("id", 4003))`) and by `editor/jobs.py:118 detect_deploy_target`, which feeds `workspace/builddoc.py` at **three** sites — `:172`, `:618`, `:982`, each `tid = self.worktree_id or 4003`. CLAUDE.md §3 gives this key its own ⚠ block, because pinning it renames the *human's* Build-tab "Test slot NNNN" radio: "it cost a playtest … their next deploy landed there instead of the in-place target they meant, and it wiped a room that was mid-playtest." That is the single most expensive documented incident attached to this file, and nothing in the code says so — the warning lives only in the brief. The cheapest durable fix is not a refactor: it is making `builddoc.py`'s three sites label the slot as pinned-and-overridable, and making `detect_deploy_target` say in its docstring that its `field_id` return is a *human-visible default*, not a private worktree setting.
- `text_block` is read by exactly one of the eight readers (`deploy_field.py:71`), even though CLAUDE.md §5 names the per-worktree pin as *the* remedy for the text-block shadow — the failure where a custom block silently overwrites a real location's dialogue across stacked folders. A pin honoured by one of eight entry points is a pin the other seven will shadow through.

**What else it costs.** `tools/deploy_battle.py:48-62` returns the pin *before* `$FF9_MOD_FOLDER`, while its module docstring at `deploy_battle.py:5` says "Mirrors tools/deploy_field.py" and `deploy_field.py:56` is `env or pin or default` — with both set, the field lands in one folder and its BattleScene in another, silently. `health.py:137` and `editor/jobs.py:113` hardcode `FF9CustomMap`, so the GUI's Setup-and-health panel and the "Deployed here" ledger — which the human reads — name a folder the deploy will not use. `cli._deploy_cfg`'s `__file__` root finds no pin from an installed wheel.

**Recommended move.** Local repairs, no consolidation. *First move:* reorder `tools/deploy_battle.py:48-62` to `env or pin or default` — one expression, and it only makes the code do what its own docstring claims. Then: `argparse.SUPPRESS` on the six `--game` subparser sites that silently discard a root-level value (`cli.py:8011, 8223, 9240, 9669, 9685`); `build.py:9884` `p[3]`→`p[4]` (the emitter at `build.py:9127` is `FieldScene <id> <area> <mapid> <name> <block>`, so the BuildError at `build.py:10031` names the donor's BG id where the Workspace names the field); a `WORLD_ID_LO/HI` branch in `hub.validate_hub` (`hub.py:241`, today accepting 9005 while `workspace/shell.py:3759` already guards the same id); route `health.py:137`, `editor/jobs.py:113` and `_cmd_world_ledger:5477` through `config.resolve_mod_folder`; delete `cli._deploy_cfg` in favour of `config._read_deploy_config()` keeping its three tails verbatim. **No emitted bytes move in any of these.**

**Do not do.** Do not collapse the eight readers into one resolver. Swapping `tools/`'s `__file__` root for config's CWD walk means `python <checkout>/tools/deploy_field.py` run from any other CWD finds no pin and falls back to shared `FF9CustomMap` + shared id 4003 — precisely the clobber that cost the playtest. Two tests pin that root deliberately (`test_repo_root.py:154`, `test_deploy_field_script.py:300`), `deploy_field.py` reads all three keys from the same file (`:56, :57, :71`), and `floorplandoc.py:1907`'s `deploy_pin_path()` is a test-isolation seam whose docstring names reading the real pin "this repo's most-recurring test defect". Equally, do not blanket-SUPPRESS the `--mod-folder` subparser sites: six of them use `default=None` as a *sentinel* feeding richer logic, and SUPPRESS turns a cosmetic bug into a wrong-target deploy. Retrofitting `summon-import`/`summon-deploy` to honour a pin is owner-gated — `cli.py:8266-8269` defers it in writing, and `studies/deploy-isolation/HANDOFF_DEPLOY_LEASES.md:191-196` already priced the consolidation at Stage 2, with the warning that folder isolation *alone* upgrades a visible black screen into a silent id collision.

### 2. The offline gate that doesn't gate — findings computed, then dropped at the seam

**What is wrong.** The kit has a real offline validator and a real `.eb` structural linter, and neither reaches the paths that matter. `build.lint_all` returns a six-slot `LintReport`; `cli.py:1181` renders five tagged slots and `deploy.py:599` renders the same literal *minus* `unknown` — the typo'd-key check whose own docstring says "a typo'd key is not an error to the build, it is silently ignored, which is the whole bug class." `grep -n warnings tools/deploy_field.py` returns zero hits, yet `build_mod` returns `"warnings": [...]` at `build.py:10177` carrying lint_logic's output. `_fieldschema.VOCAB['']` holds 82 top-level sections and 36 of the 61-element `ENFORCED` frozenset are no-dot entries, because ENFORCED is a byproduct of what the bundled examples happen to author. `_VERBATIM_IGNORED_BLOCKS` (`build.py:3284`) names one block. `content/behavior.py` is 4,141 lines with 166 `raise BehaviorError` and `build.validate` never compiles it. And 31 `.eb` "round-trip" assertions reduce to `bytes(x) == x`, because `EbScript.to_bytes()` is `return self.data` (`eb/model.py:108-109`).

**What it costs.** `python -m ff9mapkit behavior compile examples/siege/siege.field.toml` **tracebacks today** with `KeyError: 'npc'` at `cli.py:900` — `[siege]` desugars into 5 unit rows, 4 of them class rows, and the CLI seats slots per-UNIT while `behaviorscan.py:1288`, `behaviortoml.py:216` and `siege.py:775` all seat per-MEMBER. The Workspace renders the same file fine. A `[[ladder]]`, `[[platform]]`, `[[jump]]` or `[[savepoint]]` on a verbatim fork builds clean, lints clean, deploys clean and is absent in game — their injectors all live inside `build_script` (`6842`, `6895`, `6947`, `7066`), which `build.py:9085` bypasses. A `navigible = true` typo silently compiles a *different* ladder mechanism (`build.py:1674` branches the whole mechanism on `la.get("navigable")`; `build.py:1705` catches the fallthrough). And "NPC has no model/preset — it will CLONE THE PLAYER model" is computed, put on `info["warnings"]`, and thrown away by the one script CLAUDE.md §4 mandates.

**Recommended move.** *First move:* add two lines after `tools/deploy_field.py:129` printing `info["warnings"]` — it runs after `build_mod` returns and only writes to stdout, so it cannot move a byte, and it is the only deploy entry point with the gap (`tools/deploy_battle.py:284` and `deploy.py:331/638/1289` already print). Then: fix `cli.py:900` to `row_members`-based seating and add the first test that drives that verb at all; give `LintReport` a `tagged` property so `cli.py:1181` and `deploy.py:599` iterate one seam; correct `_VERBATIM_IGNORED_BLOCKS` to the genuinely unguarded set (`ladder`, `platform`, `jump`, `savepoint`, and `ate`/`object`); convert the ~20 genuinely bare `.eb` tautologies to `eblint.errors(eblint.lint_eb(out)) == []`; call a dry compile from `lint_all` *after* the walkmesh resolve (~`build.py:4121`), not beside `lint_region_overlaps` at `4111` where no routed plan exists yet; and extend `_regen_fieldschema._STUBS` (`:44`) with the eight offline-buildable sections. Only the behavior-helper consolidation touches bytes: `siege.resolve_hireable` runs inside `FieldProject.load` (`build.py:325`) and its map lands in emitted `[[choice]] requires_flag` values, so the shared helper must preserve the `row_members` order and the `900+10*ui+bi` / `890+hi` txid scheme exactly.

**Do not do.** Do not invert `_VERBATIM_IGNORED_BLOCKS` into a wired/unwired allow-list: it needs all 82 keys classified, a boolean set cannot express the conditional wiring that dominates this path (encounter, cutscene, choice, shop, prop are each *partly* wired, and `build.py:3314-3335` already says so precisely), and since `cli.py:1186` exits 1 on any warning, one mislabel is a broken build gate for every fork. The scouting list's own fallback contained four mislabels — `sps` is wired at `build.py:6248`, `qte`/`numeric_input` are already fatal validate problems at `2439`/`2468`, `shop`/`synthesis` are already warned at `9060-9070`. Do not add entry-span-overlap or fpos-inside-entry invariants to `eblint` (new production surface on a linter with a 676-field zero-error proof), do not build a nine-sink `print_build_warnings` helper (the sinks are stdout, stderr, an `out()` closure and a Tk `self.post`), and do not write `_STUBS` entries for `[[object]]`/`[[player_func]]` — they need verbatim `.eb` sidecars the provenance gate forbids, and `_EXPECTED_PARTIAL` being empty (`_regen_fieldschema.py:311`) means a stub that fails to build hard-refuses the whole schema regen for everyone.

### 3. One engine rule, many copies — including two green tests asserting opposite laws

**What is wrong.** Three engine-ABI rules each exist as 5–10 independent implementations in the byte layer, with no owner. The type-1 func-table serializer (`<tag:u16><fpos:u16>` × N, then bodies) appears at `content/{shop.py:88, platform.py:200, savepoint.py:673, jump.py:95}` as byte-identical `_assemble_entry` copies, inlined at `ladder.py:439`, `cutscene.py:130`, `object.py:50`, `region.py:414`, hand-unrolled at `savepoint.py:1128`, and a tenth time in `eb/ebsrc.py:698-713`. The entry-table splice is duplicated between `content/reinit.py:57-80` and `eb/edit.py:178-212` — whose docstring literally says it is "The re-layout content.reinit does for the after-battle handler, generalized" — and a third time in `tools/ladder_real.py:40-67`, whose docstring says "generalized from content.reinit.add_reinit". The jump-target rule is cloned from `eb/disasm.py:159-172` into `forkreport.py:450-455`.

**What it costs.** This is not hypothetical drift; the tree already exhibits it. `eb/ebsrc.py:709-710` carries an fpos-overflow guard (`if fpos > 0xFFFF: raise`) that *none* of the nine sibling copies has. `forkreport._jump_target` has no `0x02` branch and its docstring asserts the wrong ABI ("operand is a signed i16 skip distance"). Worse, that wrong law is **frozen in a passing test**: `tests/test_forkreport.py:64-65` asserts a signed backward read for `JMP_FALSE` (0x02), while `tests/test_eb.py:192` asserts the opposite and names `disasm.jump_target` as the authority. The gate suite currently defends the bug — exactly CLAUDE.md §7's "a green gate suite is a regression harness, not an oracle." Three modules also spell the entry table as bare `128` and `i * 8` (`reinit.py:59/75/77`, `eventscan.py:184/375/1029`), so no grep finds every consumer and an audit by search is impossible.

**Recommended move, and the choice between the two patterns.** Before deleting a copy, ask whether `vendor/`'s answer fits: `content/*._assemble_entry` is nine copies of a fourteen-line loop inside one importable package with no packaging reason to be self-contained — no Blender-runtime constraint, no shipped-separately boundary — so unlike `vendor/`, the copies buy nothing and a sync-gate would be a test asserting that nine files in the same package agree with each other. Here deletion is right and the gate pattern is not. (The reverse call also holds: do not "fix" `vendor/` by importing across the boundary — `test_vendor_sync.py` is the correct shape for that one.)

*First move:* substitute `ENTRY_TABLE_OFF`/`ENTRY_SLOT_SIZE` (already owned by `eb/model.py:40-41`) for the bare literals so one grep finds every consumer. Then: add `region.pack_entry_funcs(funcs, *, entry_type=REGION_ENTRY_TYPE)` holding the existing loop verbatim and delete the six copies (this also cleans `platform.py:110`, which today emits a region entry and slices the type byte back off with `[1:]`); convert `object.carry_bytes` last, because its docstring promises a byte-for-byte round-trip; collapse `reinit.add_reinit` onto `edit.add_function` (the only behavioural delta is a pure gain — `add_function` raises on an empty entry, `add_reinit` silently returns a corrupt file); and only then add the fpos guard to the single owner. **All of this is byte-emitting**, but the replacement bodies are the existing code character-for-character, so bytes are identical by construction — prove it with a throwaway `old(x) == new(x)` differ over every shape, calibrated by deliberately perturbing one copy to confirm it goes red, and run it where extracted templates exist (a bare worktree collects nothing and passes vacuously).

**Do not do.** Do not mint an `EbEntry`/`EntryBuilder` class or a `content/entrypack.py` that everything serializes through — the entire safety argument is "character-for-character", and an abstraction forfeits it. Leave `battle/ailint.py:52-63` alone: its jump rule is already *correct*, and `disasm.py:152-154` explicitly sanctions it as a guarded parallel copy. Leave both `eb/edit.py` jump inlines alone (the straddle fixup at `:322-333` independently needs the raw operand and its own `0x02` branch). Drop the proposed `binutils.sx16` export — four of its five targets are generic i16 sign-extends over expression data, not jump logic, and folding them in would bundle cosmetics into a behaviour-changing commit.

### 4. `build.py`'s god functions — 2,037 lines holding ~65 TOML schemas in one scope

**What is wrong, and how little of it is worth acting on.** `validate` spans `build.py:1239-3275`: 2,037 lines, 368 of the file's 448 `problems.append` calls, `project.raw.get` for 67 distinct sections. The scoping tax is visible in the source: `_aslist` (`1649`) and `_aslist2` (`1659`) are byte-identical two-line functions defined ten lines apart because the name was taken. Separately, the verbatim `.mes` txid ladder encodes its block order twice in load-bearing form — a seven-deep prefix-sum chain (`5486, 5542, 5606, 5639, 5698, 5749, 5915`) and a parallel set of count functions — with two unowned predicate twins (`_verbatim_prop_message_count:5717`, `_verbatim_choice_message_count:5676`) and one dead count function (`_verbatim_cutscene_message_count:5723-5728`, zero callers).

**What it costs — and the argument against fixing most of it.** This is where new TOML blocks land, so per "THE DEFECT FOLLOWS THE AUTHORSHIP" it is where defects will keep being minted. But that same law cuts against a large extraction: **nothing here is a live bug**, the twins have not drifted, and a five-step decomposition of the build gate is itself the largest single act of surface-minting this report could recommend. Rating it high/large and then executing it would be the report exempting itself from the law it quotes. So the recommendation is deliberately small.

The two items that are genuinely worth doing are the two that are *already* defects-in-waiting with a named failure mode:

1. **The unowned txid twins.** If prop's and choice's count predicates drift, the symptom is silently wrong dialogue in a shipped fork — correct flags, correct triggers, wrong text, no log. Give them a shared predicate the way `_event_shows_text` (`5523`) and `_npc_needs_default_talk` (`5500`) already do for event and npc, and delete the dead cutscene counter. No bundled example under `ff9mapkit/examples/` has a `[verbatim_eb]` block, so the golden oracles never touch that channel — which is why step zero is a no-install characterization test for the full eight-block ladder (`FieldProject(raw, tmp_path)` with no donor falls back to `CARRY_BASE_TXID`, as `test_text.py:275` and `test_logic_add.py:365` already prove).
2. **The `_aslist`/`_aslist2` twins**, and the nine copy-pasted inject→lint→raise guards with nine hand-unique `_XX_errs` names — cheap, local, and the guards hide a real hole. The helper is not what closes it: a single lint of the final `verbatim_bytes` before `build_mod` sees them is.

**If a larger extraction is ever taken on**, the safe order is known and should be recorded rather than rediscovered: hoist `_check_window_attrs` (`2777-3028`, 252 lines, 11 call sites, never references `project`, captures exactly `problems` and `_wa_npc_names`) to module level; then extract the four self-contained sections one per commit — `[[savepoint]]` (`1859-1978`), `[[equip_bonus]]` (`2254`), `[[item_effect]]` (`2285`), `[[item_text]]` (`2324`); `TextPlan` and the verbatim-ladder plan object last. The extraction pattern already exists next door — eight uniform `_validate_*(project, problems)` helpers are called at `1244-1251` — and the refactor simply stopped there.

**Byte risk, stated plainly.** `validate` emits only strings — but `build_field` (`8650-8653`) raises `BuildError` on any non-empty problems list, so `validate` *is* the build gate and a check silently lost during extraction lets an invalid project ship. The obvious mitigation "diff validate's output over the bundled examples" is blind to exactly that: the examples all validate to `[]` (`test_build.py:31/48/76/...`), and `[] == []` proves nothing. The guard that catches a dropped check is the **negative fixture set** — run `test_build`, `test_lint`, `test_content`, `test_synthesis`, `test_choice_values`, `test_ate`, `test_itemtext`, `test_texttable` per commit. `TextPlan` is byte-neutral only if no field is reordered and the mixed container types are preserved verbatim (normalizing cutscene's list or ate's dict-of-strings changes what the injectors read). The verbatim-ladder plan is genuinely **byte-affecting**: the `.mes` block order is *not* the injector call order — `build_field` fires npc@8999, choice@9000, prop@9013, event@9031, chest@9041, cutscene@9054, yet choice and prop must *land above* event and chest — so an accumulator appended in call order permutes every fork's dialogue.

**Do not do.** No registry, schema table or plugin mechanism for `validate` — an ordered call list is the whole mechanism and the eight existing helpers prove it. Drop two sub-proposals: collapsing the `from_raw` adapters (there are three real ones, and they differ materially — gauge passes `resolve_item=_items.resolve` and accumulates `g_overlay_cells`, numinput cross-checks `[[behavior.hud]]`; the "fourth" at `2531` is a bare try/except with no loop), and pushing checks *into* `content/{qte,numinput,gauge}.py` (the per-block schema is already there; what remains is project-scoped by nature, and `from_raw` is fail-fast while `validate` accumulates, so moving checks down silently changes which problems get reported). Do not reorder any call in `validate` — problem *order* is what the diff harness asserts. And note two mislabels in the original extraction list: `1859-1978` is `[[savepoint]]`, not `[[cutscene]]`; `3141-3246` is the `[cutscene]` loop, not ATE (which starts at `3105`).

### 5. Test-harness seams read the real machine — and one whole tree runs nowhere

**What is wrong.** For each piece of real-machine state, one good seam exists (`provision._user_dir(sub)`, `config._read_deploy_config(start=)`) and is shadowed by ad-hoc re-implementations. The suite's one isolation guard is then attached not to a seam but to a *derived* call site (`prefs._path`) inside *one of four* test directories. `pytest.ini:2` documents the suite as "tests/ + blender/tests/" and omits two trees: `ff9mapkit/ff9mapkit/tests/` (32 files, 578 tests) runs anyway because `testpaths = ff9mapkit` points at the parent of all three in-kit trees — so those files run in every invocation and in CI while sitting outside `tests/conftest.py`'s autouse `_isolate_prefs` and `qt_drain`. `docsite/tests/` is outside `testpaths` entirely and is covered in §6. `pyproject.toml:81-83` uses `packages.find` with setuptools' default `namespaces = true`, so `ff9mapkit.tests` ships in the wheel.

**What it costs.** An agent who adds a suite-wide guard to `tests/conftest.py` — which is what `_isolate_prefs` is, and whose docstring says "without isolation the suite would read the developer's real theme/recent-projects file" — covers 306 of 338 in-kit test modules and believes it covers all of them. The sibling seam `update_check._state_path` is covered by nobody and writes JSON into the real `%LOCALAPPDATA%`. And the ambiguity has already cost a round: `d6a9c6c` deleted the inner tree as "a stray" and `2863419` restored it as "a REAL second test tree (32 files)" — one merge from losing 10,720 lines.

**Recommended move.** *First move:* `git mv ff9mapkit/ff9mapkit/tests/*.py ff9mapkit/tests/` and rewrite `pytest.ini:2` to name every tree that exists, including the one `testpaths` excludes. The basenames are fully disjoint from the 306 and the 10, none of the 32 uses a relative import or `__file__`, and the collect count must stay identical. It also drops `ff9mapkit.tests` out of wheel discovery for free. ### 7. No static-analysis or type-checking gate exists anywhere

**What is wrong.** `ff9mapkit/pyproject.toml` contains only `[tool.setuptools.*]`. There is no `[tool.ruff]`, no `[tool.mypy]`, no `[tool.pyright]`, no `setup.cfg`, no `.ruff.toml`, no pre-commit config. `release.yml` runs `twine check`, a wheel smoke-install, and pytest — nothing else.

**What it costs, and why this is the report's own thesis pointed back at itself.** Four of the lenses this scout ran — unused names, naming consistency, complexity, dead code — are properties a linter enforces mechanically and continuously. Spending human attention on them produces nineteen hand-found items and no mechanism that catches the twentieth. ### 8. Concurrency and write-atomicity — the one axis with a hard environmental constraint, and one lane outside it

**What is wrong.** CLAUDE.md §3 elevates concurrency to an environmental fact: "Many agent worktrees run concurrently, sharing ONE game install, ONE memory store, and ONE set of mod folders. Re-verify git and deploy state before any destructive action." The repo takes this seriously — `fsutil.exclusive_fd_lock` carries a documented Windows-tearing measurement, a deliberate non-blocking-with-backoff design, and a 120s `FOLDER_LOCK_TIMEOUT` whose comment explains the tradeoff. `fsutil.py:70-72` names the failure mode in writing: "a load-bearing caller (a deploy's DictionaryPatch rewrite) catches it BY NAME and aborts loudly — proceeding unlocked is how a concurrent deploy loses another session's registration."

**One deploy lane never took the lock.** Grepping the lock owners, `locked_mod_folder` / `locked_sidecar` are held by `deploy.py`, `models/mint.py`, `models/anim.py`, `reverttmpl.py`, `tools/deploy_field.py` and `tools/deploy_battle.py` — and **not** by `summons/deploy.py`, which performs an unlocked read-modify-write of `DictionaryPatch.txt` at `summons/deploy.py:1092` and of `Memoria.ini` at `:1170`, and emits the same unlocked pattern into its generated revert script at `:1148` and `:1154`. It imports `models/mint.py`, which *does* take the lock, so it is one import away from the owner. A lost `DictionaryPatch` line is the null-`.eb` black screen.

Two other observations belong to the same lens and were not swept: `summons/deploy.alloc_mint_id` (`summons/deploy.py:1037-1053`) infers GEO-id occupancy from the presence of a `Models/*/{id}/` directory, is the only deploy path that never calls `deploystack.check_model_id_collisions` (`grep -rn deploystack` hits cli, journey, world/atlas, hub, deploylog, deploy, floorplan, campaign, build — **zero** under `summons/`), and misses `GEO_WEP_*` mints in its own folder because `models/export.model_dir_parts` (`export.py:19-25`) routes type-6 to `BattleMap/BattleModel/6/<id>/`. And `campaign.py`, `journey.py` and `world/atlas.py` did not appear in the lock grep either — each writes into a shared mod folder and each should be checked against the same contract before the next multi-session deploy round.

**Recommended move.** Wrap `summons/deploy.py`'s two registration writes (and the two in its revert template) in `locked_mod_folder`, matching `tools/deploy_field.py`'s call shape exactly — this is a re-point onto an existing owner, not new machinery, and it changes no emitted byte. Then give `alloc_mint_id` `deploystack.model_ids_at(mod_root)` plus the loud-warn guard copied from `tools/deploy_field.py:760-774`; this one *can* change which id is picked, so it ships separately and needs the first-ever test of that function. Then sweep `campaign.py` / `journey.py` / `world/atlas.py` for the same gap.

### 9. The generated-data layer — ~22.7k lines, nine generators, nothing asserts they are current

**What is wrong.** Ten checked-in tables under `ff9mapkit/ff9mapkit/` total roughly 22,700 lines — `_animdb_all.py` 14,125 (6% of the whole package), `_animdb.py` 2,994, `_fieldtable.py` 1,516, `_scenedb.py` 869, `_fieldtext.py` 845, `_modeldb.py` 725, `_npcparams.py` 634, `_bonelabeldb.py` 526, `_modelalias.py` 329, `_fieldschema.py` 144 — produced by nine `_regen_*` modules. Every generator that reads engine source requires a Memoria clone (`--memoria <path>`) that CI does not have. `ls ff9mapkit/tests | grep -iE 'regen|animdb|modeldb|fieldtable'` returns nothing; only `test_fieldschema.py`, `test_scene.py` and `test_provision.py` mention `_regen_` at all.

The headers are good as far as they go — each file opens with "DO NOT EDIT BY HAND. Regenerate with: …" and names its upstream source file. What none of them records is **which Memoria revision it was generated from.** CLAUDE.md §5 pins the engine at commit `6b8bb2d5` and the fork-gate patch stack keeps moving; nothing connects a table to the tree it was read out of, and nothing would notice if the upstream table gained a row.

**Why the other lenses cannot see this.** These files are neither dead nor duplicated. They are *unverifiable* — a distinct class from anything the duplication or dead-code lens detects, and the reason it took a separate pass to surface.

**Recommended move — a provenance header, not CI regeneration.** Regenerating in CI is the obvious wrong answer here: it would require a Memoria clone in the CI environment, which runs straight into the provenance gate's whole point. The cheap correct fix is a machine-readable header line per generated file (`# generated-from: Memoria@<sha> by _regen_animdb_all.py on <date>`) plus one test that parses all ten and asserts the header exists and is well-formed. That costs one test, makes staleness a *visible* fact rather than an invisible one, and lets a human decide when a regeneration round is due. Note that a header alone does not detect drift — it makes drift diagnosable, which is the achievable goal under this repo's constraints.

### 10. Documentation, schema and CLI can drift apart with nothing watching

**What is wrong.** The kit is published on PyPI at 1.0.0b19 with 138 CLI verbs. `docsite/uiharvest.py` generates 18+ pages from that CLI. `ff9mapkit/docs/FORMAT.md` and `BEHAVIOR.md` are hand-maintained specs of the same TOML surface that `_fieldschema.py` describes — and `_fieldschema.py` is itself *generated from the bundled examples*, so the examples are the de-facto schema. **What it costs.** It compounds with two other findings: the `docsite/` generator is ungated (§6), and `ENFORCED` covers only 36 of 82 authorable sections precisely because it is a byproduct of what the examples happen to author (§2). ### F04 in detail — why this is not a ten-line swap

The finding stands: the docstring at `forkreport.py:450` asserts an ABI the engine does not have, and `test_forkreport.py:64-65` freezes it. But three things make the replacement bigger than a delete-and-import:

- **The contracts differ.** `forkreport._jump_target` returns `-1` on failure; `eb.disasm.jump_target` returns `None`. The sole consumer is `forkreport.py:469-470`: `tgt = _jump_target(ins)` then `k = _bisect.bisect_left(offs, tgt) if tgt >= 0 else i + 1`. `None >= 0` raises `TypeError`, so the guard must be rewritten in the same commit.
- **Expression operands change hands.** `disasm.jump_target` returns `None` whenever `ins.arg_is_expr[0]`; `_jump_target` currently reads those bytes as a literal. Swapping changes behavior on every expression-operand jump, independently of the signedness rule.
- **The divergent case is common, not exotic.** It is not "an SC-gated `JMP_IFNOT` skipping >32KB inside one real `Main_Init`". It is **every backward `JMP_FALSE`** (`raw >= 0x8000`). Today those yield a negative target and fall through via the `tgt >= 0` guard; under the correct unsigned rule they become huge forward targets that `bisect_left` pushes past `n`, terminating the walk. "Fall through to `i+1`" and "terminate" produce different `InitObject` lists.

So the gate is **not** "diff the reports; they must be byte-identical" — that would assert something there is no reason to expect and would read a correct landing as a failure. The gate is: run `fork-report` on several donor ids before and after, diff, and **account for every difference** — each one should trace to a backward `JMP_FALSE` or an expression operand, and the post-change slot list should be the defensible one.
