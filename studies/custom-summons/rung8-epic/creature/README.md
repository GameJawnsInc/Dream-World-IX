## 3. THE CLIP CONTRACT — what the sequence/wiring lane consumes

**Settled, and not by this lane.** STORYBOARD §6.4 listed K2 (`clips = [<paths>]`) as pending work;
the kit lane **landed it while the creature was being built**, so the answer is read off shipped
code:

- `summons/deploy.py:authored_clip_paths` takes the authored branch for non-digit entries;
- `clip_key_of` gives a **named** stem the key `AUTHORED_CLIP_KEY_BASE + index` = **60000 + i**
  (a *numeric* stem like `0.anim` would instead be taken at its word and leave the mint band);
- `clip_name_map` lets `[[summon.staging.play]] clip = "emerge"` resolve to it.

So: **ship named clips, let the kit mint the keys.** An earlier draft here hedged with a parallel
numeric set; that was removed once the kit's rule was read — shipping `0.anim` would have opted out
of the mint band silently.

| clip | file the TOML names | kit key | on disc |
|---|---|---|---|
| emerge | `nimbra/emerge.anim` | 60000 | `Animations/6400/60000.anim` |
| drift | `nimbra/drift.anim` | 60001 | `Animations/6400/60001.anim` |
| strike | `nimbra/strike.anim` | 60002 | `Animations/6400/60002.anim` |

`../stage/creature/CLIPS.json` carries all of it in machine-readable form. **No `3DModelAnimation`
line** — the SFX route loads by literal asset path, so clips are recast-only; only
`3DModel 6400 GEO_MON_B0_M400` needs the one relaunch.

**Verified by integration, not by prose:** `make_nimbra_anims.py` **reads** the playlist and window out
of `bench/rung8.field.toml` (one source of truth — the 2026-07-24 retime found three private copies of
the tick table and this was one of them), then calls the kit's *own* `anim_frame_count` and
`playlist_coverage` on the emitted files and cross-checks its own arithmetic against them — 145 ticks,
`short_by 0`. `build_rung8_stage.py --clean --check` reports *"all real — no placeholders"* and
`CHECK CLEAN`, with the emitted manifest playlist reading `['60000@1','60003@1','60002@1','60001@1']`.

> **THE RETIME DID TOUCH THIS LANE — AND HAD TO.** §11's first pass re-cut the cast from 32.3 s to 9.3 s
> purely by moving the playlist's `Speed` divisors, on the belief that a divisor slows a clip down.
> **It does not** — `SFXDataMesh.cs:869` divides a clip-frame index by a tick count, so `Speed > 1` runs
> the clip out after `1/s` of its entry and freezes the rig (STORYBOARD **§11.9**, measured by
> `playlist_sim.py`). The fix is this lane's: **every entry is Speed 1 and the CLIP is sized to its
> beat.** `drift` (N=75) is **byte-identical**; `emerge` (90 → 15) and `strike` (60 → 30) are the same
> authored envelopes resampled by `nimbra_clips.rescale`; `driftlook` (N=25) is `build_drift`'s own
> normalized cycle at the look's length. The seam + loop assertions re-ran green on all four.

## 4. THE OFFLINE EYE — what each pass actually caught

The rung-7 "red circle" incident is why this is mandatory. Six passes, six real defects; **none of
them were visible in the numbers**, and every one of them would have cost a playtest.

| pass | what the eye showed | fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a **mushroom/jellyfish**: the closed-revolution cowl swallowed the core column whole | cowl → an **open cape** (≈292° arc, front gap) so the torso reads through it |
| 2 | arms **punching through the cloth**, rendered as a sawtooth | cowl hem capped, arms moved outboard |
| 3 | arms now **detached cones** floating beside the body | re-read the storyboard's ASCII: the yoke is **short and wide**, the arms take over the outline below it. Cowl 300-tall→175 (247 with the hunched back collar); arms attach at its corners |
| 4 | **open tube ends** at both shoulders (a hole where the arm meets the yoke) | shoulder caps + ribbon root caps |
| 5 | `emerge` frame 0 was **a bird with its wings out**, not crossed arms | a **sign error**: crossing the left arm (x<0) toward the midline needs a *negative* Z rotation, so the angle is `+55*side`, not `-55*side` |
| 6 | the shoulder **tore away from the yoke** under the strike's 47° wind-back; and `drift` was invisible across six sampled frames | bleed `bone002` into the arm's top 22 % of rows; raise the veil wave 8°→15° (a veil bone sits mid-ribbon, so 15° ≈ 95u of tip travel) |

The **Blender leg earns its place** on pass 2 specifically: `preview.render_model` is a painter's
sort, so interpenetration and "is that a hole?" are unanswerable there. Workbench with `light='FLAT'`
+ an emission material is also the only render that tells the truth about brightness, because the SFX
path has **no battle-actor lighting pass** (rung-7 residual b) — it is the render that judges
STORYBOARD §1.5's "author ~15 % darker than you want". Two framing bugs were paid for en route and
are now documented in the script: Blender's default **100-unit far clip** cannot see a scene authored
in FF9 units, and a *pitched* camera parked at the target height aims `dist*tan(pitch)` below it.

Look at: `renders/kit_turntable_sheet.png` (8 angles), `renders/blender_bind_*.png` (the truthful
ones), `renders/kit_clip_{emerge,drift,strike}.png` (6 frames each).

## 5. THE DEVIATIONS FROM THE STORYBOARD — all of them, flagged not buried

§1.3's table is a *table*; §1.2's ASCII is a *drawing*. Where they disagreed the drawing won, because
the drawing is the thing a player sees. Each deviation is commented at its own line in
`nimbra_spec.py`.

| # | Storyboard | Built | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | cowl **300 tall** | 175 front (247 incl. the hunched back collar) | at 300 the cape reached −850 and hid the core column *and* the arms (eye passes 1 + 3). The ASCII's yoke is 2 rows with the arms below it. |
| D2 | cowl **520 wide**, "widest point" | 504; the **arms** reach ±260 and are widest by ~8u | keeps the arms clear of the cloth at every height (eye pass 2). |
| D3 | core `−1080 → −740`, 210→90 dia | `−1140 → −740`, 224→90 | the column has to be *seen* through the yoke's opening — the ASCII draws it as a continuous `\|`. |
| D4 | arm 620 long, hangs from −1080 | bones span −1050→−470; **visible** geometry 565 | the shoulder is inside the yoke; only what emerges is drawn. |
| D5 | *"Every clip's first frame AND last frame must be the shared drift rest pose"* | `emerge` frame 0 is the **folded** pose | §1.7 self-conflicts: the same paragraph requires emerge to *start* "collapsed inward… arms crossed". Emerge frame 0 is the only clip boundary in the playlist with **no predecessor** (it is manifest frame 0, at Scaling 0.15, inside a full blackout) — there is no seam there to pop. **Every real seam is exact to 0.0**, machine-checked in `validate_nimbra.py`. |
| D6 | drift veil wave (amplitude unspecified) | 15°/9° (was 8°/5.5°) | at 8° the wave was invisible across six sampled frames — for the clip that carries 12 of the cast's 32 seconds that is a failure, not restraint. **The specified numbers were kept**: root bob ±14u, mask counter-rotation ±5°. |
| D7 | part vert budgets | mask/cowl/arms/core/veil = 62/266/356/169/840 | shape-driven; total is under both the ~2100 estimate and the 7000 ceiling. |

## 6. Residuals / watch items for the cast

1. **The yaw is still the one knob that may cost a cast** (STORYBOARD R4). NIMBRA's face is at model
   **+Z**; the manifest ships the proven `(0, 180, 180)` baseline. If cast 1 shows it facing away,
   flip every Rotation `Y` 180↔0 (and 168↔12) — a one-line manifest edit, recast-only.
2. **Brightness is judged, not measured.** The Blender eye renders unlit against a near-black world
   and NIMBRA reads as a pale grey-green figure with a bright plate — which is the design. The
   `DARKEN = 0.85` factor is the only lever if it blows out in-game; it is one constant in
   `nimbra_spec.py` and needs no re-export of anything but the PNG.
3. **The mask is small** (140u wide against a 545u shoulder span — as specified). Its readability at
   battle distance rests entirely on the amber rim ring + eye hollows, which were authored
   deliberately generous for that reason. If it disappears in-game, widen `MASK_HW/MASK_HH` before
   touching anything else.
4. **`bone003` (neck) carries no weight and that is correct** — the mask floats, there is no neck
   geometry. It is still a real driven `_smr.bones` entry (the m1b node-index law) and it *is* keyed
   by `emerge`/`strike`, so nothing about it is accidental. Both validators assert it explicitly so a
   future reader does not "fix" it.
5. **The veil's striations still read slightly like fabric** in the software preview. They are much
   softer in the unlit Blender render, which is the truthful one. Not worth another pass offline.
