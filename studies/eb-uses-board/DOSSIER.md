# `.eb` uses — the full dossier

Every idea the brainstorm produced, with the complete agent reasoning behind it. The ranked, readable version is [`BOARD.md`](BOARD.md); this is the evidence underneath it. Background briefs the idea agents worked from: [`BRIEFS.md`](BRIEFS.md). Where to start next: [`HANDOFF.md`](HANDOFF.md).

**How it was produced.** 4 grounding readers → 14 ideation lenses → a per-idea reality+novelty screen → two adversarial verifiers per idea (an engine expert and a project historian, each told to refute) → a completeness critic → 4 gap-filling lenses whose ideas went straight to the two verifiers. An idea was killed if the screen failed it, or if BOTH verifiers refuted it. One refutation alone did not kill; those caveats are recorded in the idea's detail. Reconstructed from the workflow journal (494 agent results, every verdict matched to its idea).

**Totals:** 173 unique ideas · **124 survived** · 49 killed (8 at screen, 41 by both verifiers). BOARD.md's "40 killed" counts round 1 only; round 2 lost 9 more.

**Read with care.** Every agent could read the repo and none could play the game. A "survived" verdict means two skeptics could not knock it down from source, not that it works (CLAUDE.md §7).

## Completeness critic

The territories the critic said round 1 had missed. Round 2 (`gap-1`…`gap-4`) worked these.

1. **Battle-side `.eb` — the member-list expression lane and the field↔battle seam.** Every one of the 100 ideas lives on the field side; not one spends §18.7 or the `_A`/`_E` operator family, and the brief hands over an enormous unclaimed surface: `B_MEMBER(N)` selectors where `geo_id`, `mesh`, `motion` and `pos.x/ny/z` are *writable*, `B_LMAX`/`B_LMIN` as party ARGMAX/ARGMIN over a bitmask, `B_SELECT` as a random member pick, `B_SYSLIST[0/1]` for target/self, plus `BattleDialog`, `ContinueBattleMusic`, `TerminateBattle`, `RunBattleCode`, `AddBattleStatus`. An ideator here asks: what is a *boss as a stage*? Scripted positional choreography mid-fight (writable pos), a phase change that swaps the model and animation without a new enemy record, a puzzle-boss whose solution is a field flag you set before entering, a fight you win by talking, a battle that is really a cutscene with an escape hatch, an AI that reads `gEventGlobal` (same heap, Global source) so the boss knows what you did three fields ago, and `TerminateBattle` as a narrative verb ("the fight is interrupted"). Also the reverse seam: battle AI *writing* story state so the field learns how the fight went.

2. **The concurrency and process model — `.eb` as a multi-threaded runtime.** The dispatch family (`RunScriptAsync`/`RunScript`/`RunScriptSync`, the unmodeled object-indexed twins `0x16/0x18/0x1A`, `RunSharedScript`/`WaitSharedScript`/`StopSharedScript`, `CreateObject`, `SetObjectIndex`, `SetObjectFlags`, `TerminateEntry`, `ReturnEntryFunctions`) is a cooperative, non-preemptive, per-object scheduler that the 100 ideas touch exactly once, in passing, in the watchdog idea — and even there only as a happy accident. Nobody has proposed a *runtime discipline*: a daemon convention with declared tick budgets, a message bus so objects talk instead of squatting on shared GLOB bytes, a supervisor tree that restarts a wedged object, priorities and a time-sliced scheduler so six behavior trees and a HUD tick coexist without frame cost, an object POOL (fixed N objects recycled through `SetModel` + `Show`/`Hide`) to fake crowds, swarms, projectiles or particles-as-entities, and the deadlock/starvation analysis that `RunScriptSync` plus `WaitSharedScript` obviously invites. The kit has a compiler and a data layer; it has no operating system.

3. **Input as a verb system — the `B_KEY*` family and the controller as an expressive channel.** FF9 fields ship exactly one player verb: Confirm. The expression layer exposes five pure per-frame button reads plus the impure `B_KEYON`, and `AddControllerMask`/`RemoveControllerMask` can subtract axes and buttons at will — and the ideas set spends this on one photo-mode poller, one control-twist status effect, and a nod at stock's one-button tutorial. The missing territory is *new verbs*: charge-and-release, hold-to-act with a filling gauge, double-tap dash, chorded and sequenced combos, timing windows (parry, rhythm, a QTE that accepts exactly one button for exactly twelve frames), mash meters, a context-sensitive action button whose meaning is computed from `B_BGIID`, an in-field input recorder for macros/attract-mode demos/TAS-style replay, and player-facing remapping. Adjacent and equally unclaimed: the assist surface — one-button play, hold-vs-toggle, adjustable text speed, a universal skip/pause service, and a photosensitivity mode that damps the `FadeFilter` flashes several ideas cheerfully propose.

4. **Actor puppetry and compositing — the attach/scale/focus/trig band.** The ideas set uses actors as bodies that walk and talk. The substrate says an actor is a rig: `AttachObject`/`DetachObject`/`AttachObjectOffset` (parent one object to another with an offset), `SetModel` for a live model swap, `SetObjectSize` with four operands (non-uniform scale), `SetObjectLogicalSize`, `SetModelColor`, `SetAnimationSpeed`/`SetAnimationInOut`/`MakeAnimationLoop`, `EnableHeadFocus`/`SetHeadFocusMask`/`FollowFocus`, plus the entirely unexplored parameterized `RunModelCode(1,2,2,2)` — and, in the expression layer, `B_SIN`/`B_COS`/`B_ANGLE`/`B_DISTANCE` for motion computed rather than keyframed. Only the shadow opcodes got an idea. An ideator here builds: compound creatures assembled from attached parts (a segmented worm, a floating boss with orbiting satellites, a marionette on strings); transformation and growth as a scaled tween; a "prop rig" where a cart, a lift or a raft is literally an actor carrying attached actors; procedural motion — orbits, pendulums, sine-float, spiral ascents, wave-propagating crowds; time dilation via global animation speed; and a modelled-not-scripted crowd whose heads all track the player.

5. **The meta / fourth-wall register — the game reading and mutating its own identity and save infrastructure.** Every one of the 100 ideas is diegetic-within-fiction or developer-facing. None occupies the Undertale/DDLC/OneShot register, and FF9's substrate is unusually well armed for it: `Menu(1,char)` is a real text input that renames a *real character*, `SetName` writes one, `SetFieldName` makes the menu and the save slot lie about where you are, `DeleteAllCards` and `ClearMemoriaVector`/`ClearMemoriaDictionary` let the game destroy its own persistent data in front of you, `B_SYSVAR[20]` is total play time, `[19]` is your card count, `B_SYSVAR[16]` is the frog counter, `TREASURE_HUNTER_POINTS` is a popcount over your flag history, and `Menu(4,0)` makes saving itself a scripted act. Ideas that live here: an NPC who knows how long you have played and how many times you saved in this room; a character who takes the name you typed and refuses to give it back; a save slot that reports a lie, then a truth; a death that persists across a reload because the ledger outlived the load; a mod that reacts to being reinstalled. Also the honest inverse — where the line is, and what a mod owes a player whose save it touches.

6. **Character-sheet surgery as systems design — builds, jobs, drafts and status-as-traversal.** The brief lists `SetCharacterLevel`/`Exp`/`Equipment`, `SetCharacterData`, `LearnAbility`/`UnlearnAbility`, `SetHP`/`SetMP`, `CureStatus`, `AddCharacterAttribute` (bit 4 = LADDER), `SetRow`, `Party`/`RemoveParty`/`SetPartyReserve`, `AddBattleStatus`/`RemoveBattleStatus`, and `Battle` with a computed scene id — a complete progression-mutation API reachable from any field. The ideas set spends it on exactly two things: a permadeath warden and an NG+ capsule (both *carryover* framings). Nothing designs a *system* with it. An ideator here proposes: a job/class system where a trainer NPC grants and revokes ability sets, respec at a cost in gil; a roguelike run where you draft abilities between fights from a vector-backed pool and the ladder is data; an arena that normalizes the party to a fixed level so a challenge is authored, not scaled around; equipment durability or a loadout enforced per scene; mercenaries hired per dungeon with a gil upkeep clock; and the one the brief names and nobody developed — **status as a traversal verb**: Float to cross lava, Mini to fit a gap, Petrify to survive a crushing room, a permanent story-curse the party carries between fields. That last one turns the battle status table into a field-puzzle vocabulary and costs no art at all.
