## 1. The Clockwork Absence

**Status:** SURVIVED · **Effort:** medium · **Engine:** stock · **Round:** 1

**Pitch.** A world that advances while you are elsewhere, without ever running a tick while you are elsewhere. On field entry, read how much play time has passed since the last stamp and integrate the whole gap in CLOSED FORM — one division, no loop.

**Mechanism (as proposed).** `B_SYSVAR[20]` is play time in seconds (engine-clamped 8,388,607 — comfortably inside the 26-bit envelope) and is read by NOTHING in the kit today (verified: zero `B_SYSVAR[20]` hits package-wide). Main_Init emits: `SET({flex(20,2)[CLK][0] ...})` reading the last stamp out of a gScriptVector cell, `elapsed = B_SYSVAR[20] − stamp`, `steps = elapsed B_DIV const(period)`, then per state cell `cell B_LET (cell + rate*steps)` with a `B_LMIN`-free manual clamp (`B_GT` + `JMP_IF` to a saturate arm — NOT B_LMAX/B_LMIN, which are party-member ARGMAX and a documented trap). Finally `stamp B_LET (stamp + steps*period)` so the remainder is not lost. No in-tick loop, so the ~200-iterations/tick frame budget and the unbounded-loop hang are both dodged. THE SEED TRAP that makes this non-obvious: behavior.py's law is `size←0 / size←n` on every Main_Init to keep stale saves inert — a world-sim vector must invert that, seeding ONLY when `flex(21,1) B_VECTOR_SIZE == 0`, guarded by a `JMP_IFNOT` (forward, unsigned, legal) around the seed block, or every doorway wipes the world.
