WHERE IT ACTUALLY BREAKS (mechanism, not substrate):
1. **`B_POST_PLUS` then `B_REM` cannot wrap the stored head.** `B_REM` is a pure binary operator — it produces a value on the CalcStack and writes nothing. `B_POST_PLUS` has *already committed* the unwrapped increment. As written, head runs monotonically past N and every subsequent post writes out of range (fail-soft = silently dropped mail). The wrap needs a compound store: `Global.Byte[h] (Global.Byte[h] const(1) B_PLUS) const(N) B_REM B_LET`, or `B_REM_LET`(49) as a second statement. Not fatal, but the pitch's stated bump is wrong.
2. **head/tail alone is full-vs-empty ambiguous.** Drain-on-`head B_NE tail` means a ring that wraps to full reads as EMPTY and loses the entire buffer silently. Needs a reserved empty slot (capacity N-1) or a third count byte.
3. **One tail cannot do one-to-many fan-out.** The first consumer to drain bumps the shared tail and the message is gone for everyone else. The pitch claims fan-out; the stated two-byte mechanism is a single-consumer queue. Fan-out needs a per-consumer tail plus a min-tail (or refcount) reclaim.
4. **The stated discipline annihilates the headline benefit.** "Seed head=tail=0 and size←0 at field init" is the kit's table law (`content/behavior.py:1000-1012` — re-seeded at every Main_Init AND ~ Reload, explicitly so a stale save can't leave a tail behind). But benefit (1) is *cross-field, save-surviving* delivery. If the consumer field seeds at init, yesterday's mail is wiped before it can be read. Those are mutually exclusive as written.

TWO MORE HAZARDS THE PITCH DOESN'T ADMIT:
- The gScriptVector id namespace is **save-GLOBAL with no registry**, and the kit auto-allocates behavior tables from `TABLE_ID_BASE = 1000` in declaration order (`behavior.py:715, 1462-1486`). A mailbox id landing in that band gets `size←0`'d by some *unrelated* field's Main_Init. M must be pinned outside the auto band and that must be enforced, not documented.
- The packed message at ~2^25 sits on `EXPR_VALUE_MAX` with zero headroom, and the compiler's own fence `ADJUST_MAG_MAX = 1_000_000` (~2^20, `behavior.py` adjust `__post_init__`) would *reject* it. So the ring lives in hand-authored `.eb`, outside the `[behavior]` vocabulary and outside `behavior lint`. That's a real cost.

Also minor: `flex(22,2)` is `B_DICTIONARY`, a different container — citing it as a vector read is sloppy (`_exprtable.py:70`).

A drain-and-dispatch still needs one body per verb. The ring buys ordering and deferral, not code size.
