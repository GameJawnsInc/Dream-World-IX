## 1. The Held Two-Channel Grade

**Status:** SURVIVED · **Effort:** small · **Engine:** stock · **Round:** 1

**Pitch.** FadeFilter is not a transition — it is two independent, persistent, per-channel full-screen shader globals that HOLD until rewritten. Stop using it as a bracket and start using it as a colour grader: one opcode pair gives a field a moonlit, sepia, sickly-green or storm-grey look that survives walking, talking and menus.

**Mechanism (as proposed).** `FadeFilter` 0xEC = `SceneDirector.InitFade((mode & 2) ? Sub : Add, frames, r,g,b)` — verified at `studies/overworld-topography/scene-ladder/rung1c_handshake.py:53` and `scene-ladder/README.md:93`; the intensity operand is READ AND DISCARDED (`content/event.py:74-78`, DoEventCode.cs:632-648). Bit 1 of mode picks the channel, and the two channels are separate globals that lerp over `frames` and then HOLD (proven: round 1 of rung 1c hardlocked on a mode-0 white wash that never came back). So a grade is a PAIR held simultaneously: `FadeFilter(2,60,0,48,40,8)` subtracts warm (leaves a cool dark room) + `FadeFilter(0,60,0,0,0,18)` adds blue lift. Net transform is out = scene - SUB + ADD, per channel, script-tunable. Restore is the stock `(3,16,0,0,0,0)` sub-of-zero plus its add-channel twin. Ship as a `[grade]` block with named presets emitted once in `Main_Init` and re-emitted on story-flag change. THE ONE REAL TRAP: the fader is a UI panel drawn above dialog depth 54-68, so a HELD grade tints every window too — a graded field must make `raise` default on every window OPEN, once, honouring the raise-saturation clamp at 90 (`eb/opcodes.py:431-446`). Also bench mode 1 (add-channel restore) — it is by-construction from the handler, not from the census.

**Mechanism (screen correction).** Mechanism is right; one gap must be designed in or the feature dies on first playtest. A held grade is NOT stable, because every shipped bracket in the kit RESTORES TO ZERO, not to the grade: `(3,16,0,0,0,0)` is a sub-of-zero lerp that wipes the SUB channel outright, and the flash pair in `behavior.py:648-649` wipes the ADD channel. So any dialogue dim, savepoint save-flash, behavior flash, platform/ladder ride, gateway/worldmap warp, or death-rule exit silently ends the grade mid-field. Second, bench mode 1 before shipping it as the add-channel restore, and verify a grade survives a real battle round-trip and a menu open — the fader is a field UI panel and may or may not be reset by the battle scene swap. Keep the emission ONE pair per state change (not per frame), and keep the raise-on-open default clamped per `eb/opcodes.py:436-446`.

## 2. Overlay Lights: A Shader From Two PNGs

**Status:** SURVIVED · **Effort:** medium · **Engine:** stock · **Round:** 1

**Pitch.** Author one flat-white full-screen PNG at the additive blend shader and one at the subtractive, then drive both with SetTileColor from a tick daemon. You get a dimmable coloured LIGHT and a dimmable DARKNESS that affect the BACKGROUND ONLY — which FadeFilter physically cannot do, because it is a UI panel over everything.

**Mechanism (as proposed).** A `.bgx` OVERLAY carries an explicit `Shader:` directive; `extract.py:1346-1356` mirrors Memoria's own exporter — `PSX/FieldMap_Abr_None` opaque, `_Abr_1` ADDITIVE, `_Abr_2` SUBTRACTIVE, `_Abr_0` average, `_Abr_3` add-quarter (BGSCENE_DEF.cs:611, importer honours it at :321). `SetTileColor` 0x59 = `EBG_overlaySetShadeColor`, rgb/128 on the overlay material with 128 neutral (`content/gauge.py:20-24`), and Memoria overlays own a MeshRenderer so the shade path applies — gauge.py:313 already emits it with all four operands expression-flagged. So: a flat-white `Abr_1` overlay shaded to (20,20,60) ADDS a dim blue = a light you can dial to zero; a flat-white `Abr_2` overlay shaded up = darkness you can dial. Two 0x59 per tick from one seated daemon entry (gauge.py's ticker shape: single tag-0 func, internal `Wait(1)` loop, `allocate 2` entry locals for phase). Grade the ACTORS separately with `SetModelColor` 0x8F — uid is operand 0 (`eventscan.py:558`) — so you can light the character warm while the room goes cold, a rim mismatch the fader can never produce. Overlays are per-`CameraId`, so a multi-camera field gets a different light rig per camera for free.

**Mechanism (screen correction).** Two fixes to the mechanism as pitched. (1) "BACKGROUND ONLY" is a property of the overlay's DEPTH, not of being an overlay: overlays participate in the per-tile depth split against models (`_depth_groups` in extract.py), so a full-screen light/dark quad placed at a near depth will also wash over the characters, exactly like the fader. Author the rig at a depth behind every actor to get the background-only effect the pitch claims, and expose the depth as the knob that chooses "room only" vs "everything". (2) The actor-grading half needs a neutral check before it is trusted: field GEO models are unlit and force-assigned `Unlit/Transparent Cutout`, and the studies note `_Color` neutral is 0.5 grey (fldchar.cs:72 / FieldMapActor.cs:93), so 0x8F's neutral and its headroom above neutral are not automatically the overlay's 128 — measure one stock field's CHRCOLOR write before claiming warm-rim-vs-cold-room. Also budget it honestly: two 0x59 plus N 0x8F per tick sit in the same seated daemon as `[[gauge]]`/`[[behavior.hud]]`, so the rig should reuse gauge.py's single tag-0 ticker rather than mint a second one.

## 8. The Shadow Is The Light

**Status:** SURVIVED · **Effort:** small · **Engine:** stock · **Round:** 1

**Pitch.** FF9 gives every actor a blob shadow with scriptable size, offset, rotation-lock and amplifier — five opcodes, all unused. Compute the offset from the player's distance to a painted lamp and the shadow stretches and swings correctly as he walks past it. Dynamic lighting with zero art.

**Mechanism (as proposed).** Place a notional light at (LX, LZ) matching the painted lamp/window in the background. Per tick in the seated daemon: `dx = (obj(250).f[0] - LX) >> 4`, `dz = (obj(250).f[2] - LZ) >> 4` via the proven B_OBJSPECA reader (rung1c uses this exact form), pushed as expression operands into `SetShadowOffset` 0x82 (2,2 — signed i16, so the shadow falls AWAY from the lamp on both sides). `SetShadowSize` 0x81 (1,1) takes `base + ((|dx|+|dz|) >> k)` so the blob stretches with distance. `LockShadowRotation` 0x83 pins it so the shadow does not spin with the actor's facing — which is precisely the thing that makes stock blob shadows read as fake. `SetShadowAmplifier` 0x85 dims it as the light recedes; `DisableShadow` 0x80 kills it under an overhang. Field coordinates are low thousands, so every intermediate is far inside the 26-bit envelope. Cost: 2-3 opcodes/tick per lit actor, no art, no new flags. Pairs directly with idea #2 — the same daemon that dials the light overlay dials the shadow it casts.

**Mechanism (screen correction).** Three fixes before building.
