LENS: geometry-pipeline
================================================================================
SUMMARY: The geometry core here is stronger than its size suggests, and I want to say so before the findings. I read the 1,246 `.ff9mesh` files currently deployed to the live install and re-derived their structural properties independently of the toolkit: **zero** unindexed-contract violations, **zero** malformed headers, **zero** trailing-byte corruption, and — after calibrating against all 260 real disc-1 blocks — an engine-rejected-triangle rate on custom Terrain (502/45,127 = 1.1%) that is *below* stock's own (3,439/83,939 = 4.1%). The worst-looking custom blocks turned out to be byte-faithful carries of stock blocks (0,0) and (17,2). The sea-normal law is honoured in practice: 254,915 of 255,385 deployed Sea4 vertices carry the measured byte constant (-0.1211, 0.9785, 0.1665) and **none** carry the naive (0,1,0). That is a genuinely clean pipeline, and the reason is architectural, not lucky: every builder funnels through one write seam (`mesh.write_ff9mesh`) and every transform rebuilds a fresh triangle soup rather than mutating an indexed mesh, which makes `vcount == icount` true by construction. `meshedit.py` is best-practice work — each law enforced at the call site, each with the falsification that motivated it recorded inline, each covered by a test that fails when the law is removed. The generators fail closed (`island.landmass` refuses to deploy on `not report["clean"]`; `interior` raises on 30+ distinct geometry conditions). Where the practice is weak is not the mesh math — it is the **boundary layer**: the deployed artifact carries no identity, the primary automatic walkability gate is measurably blind to small holes (I broke it: a single deleted triangle on a real 647-tri block leaves the census green 3 times in 12), the engine's part-registration order — the single most consequential ordering fact in the whole simulator — is hardcoded four times in three mutually inconsistent orders, and the one authoring path that puts a human's hands on the geometry (the Blender OBJ round-trip) is also the one with essentially no validation and a documented, unguarded data-loss mode. Fix the boundary and this pipeline is professional-grade.

--- [geometry-pipeline#1] (critical/medium) The placement census — the only automatic walkability gate — is measurably blind to small coverage holes, and its resolution cannot simply be raised ---
CURRENT: One gate (`placement.census`) is the toolkit's only automatic, always-on offline check for the documented 'void hole = stranded player / invisible vehicle wall' defect class. It samples a 60x60u interior grid at 2.6u pitch, never touches the block frame, and — demonstrably — passes clean over single-triangle holes about a quarter of the time. Two of the three generators (island, interior) score it absolutely, so they cannot raise the pitch without tripping on geometry stock itself contains.
NORM: Coverage/watertightness validation in a shipped terrain pipeline is analytic (edge-manifold audit, boundary-cycle extraction, per-cell area accounting), not sampled; sampling is used only as a cheap smoke test on top. When sampling is used, its detection floor is stated and the acceptance threshold is differential against a reference build, not absolute. Ray/point queries run against a spatial index (uniform grid or BVH), so raising sample density is a non-decision.
REC: Three changes, in order. (1) Make the acceptance criterion DIFFERENTIAL everywhere, not just in transplant: promote transplant's 'introduced vs inherited miss' logic (transplant.py:2938-2947) into `placement` as a first-class `census_delta(built, reference)` and adopt it at island.py:806 and interior.py:2458. This is the project's own PEER GATE law applied to coverage. (2) Add a 4u bucket grid to `place()` — the mesh is already on that lattice, so it is ~20 lines and cuts per-sample work by roughly two orders of magnitude; that makes a 1u-pitch, full-part census cost well under a second per block. (3) Replace the sampled hole test as the PRIMARY gate with an analytic one: `meshedit.boundary_cycles`/`vertex_components` already extract edge-manifold boundary rings (meshedit.py:990-1022) — run them over the assembled per-part soup and assert the boundary is exactly the expected outer ring plus known apertures. That detects a one-triangle hole with certainty, at zero sampling risk, and reuses code that already exists and is tested. Keep the sampled census as the cheap smoke test it actually is, and change its docstring to state its measured detection floor rather than 'MISS must be 0 everywhere'.

--- [geometry-pipeline#5] (medium/small) The write-seam guard set is narrower than the engine's own loader, and it is an `assert` rather than a raise ---
CURRENT: The kit's single write chokepoint is well-chosen and correctly placed — the design is right — but it enforces one of the four conditions the engine's own reader enforces, and it enforces that one with a statement the interpreter can be told to skip.
NORM: The cooker validates every precondition the runtime loader checks, at the point of writing, with a hard failure — the cooker is the last place a bad asset can be stopped cheaply, and mirroring the loader's bounds is table stakes.
REC: Convert the assert to `raise ValueError` and mirror the loader: reject `vcount <= 0`, `vcount > 65535`, `icount > vcount * 3`, and any out-of-range index. Add two cheap structural checks in the same pass, both of which cost one loop over the triangles you are already serialising: a NaN/inf scan on every channel (a NaN vertex is unbounded damage in the engine and invisible to every existing gate) and a degenerate-triangle count surfaced as a warning with the stock baseline (MEASURED: 2 degenerates in 83,939 stock disc-1 triangles, so anything above single digits is anomalous). Keep it to invariants the ENGINE enforces plus arithmetic sanity — do not put look/shape judgements here; this project's own record shows those belong upstream and warn-by-default.

LENS: tools-ux
================================================================================
SUMMARY: This is an unusually strong offline geometry pipeline hobbled by a single missing organ: it cannot show you anything. Take the praise seriously first, because it is load-bearing for the recommendations. The data model is disciplined — one canonical `BlockMesh` (`extract.py:182-219`) that every one of the 29 modules speaks, with the raw Unity buffers retained for lossless re-encode. The two checks that guard the actual byte-write boundary are enforced where they belong and cannot be skipped by a new caller: the unindexed-contract assert (`mesh.py:71-74`) and the off-grid refusal (`mesh.py:43-57`, called from both deploy paths), with the module docstring explicitly explaining that low-layer placement is deliberate. `placement.py` is a byte-exact reproduction of the engine ground query and — contrary to the usual pattern in this codebase — its MISS gate is genuinely wired to raise at the call sites (`island.py:1000`, `interior.py:2459`), not merely documented. `--dry-run` reaches 21 of 32 verbs. Best of all, `transplant.py:2937-2970` gets the hardest thing right: it judges misses DIFFERENTIALLY, admitting the ones the donor itself inherits and rejecting only introduced ones — the "compare against the neighbour, not the marginal" principle that the project paid thirteen playtests to learn, already encoded in production code. And the Workspace World tab (`workspace/worlddoc.py`) is a well-built, correctly-scoped read-only atlas with a disciplined no-I/O-uninvited construction law. This team knows how to build instruments. That is precisely why the central finding stings: the toolkit authors a textured 3D world and ships zero ability to render a triangle. The only engine-faithful rasterizer — 489 lines, UNLIT because the engine binds no normal, with a per-pixel triangle-owner buffer for seam forensics — lives in `studies/path-d-new-world/render_gate.py` hardcoded to one disc, one mod folder, six bench cells and six literal camera positions from the V-shore corner. The cost is measured, not hypothetical: eight rounds chasing a ground-junction defect with zero images taken, and three defect classes closing in a single day once stills finally appeared. The human is not merely the playtest oracle here — a defensible constraint — the human is the *renderer*, which is not. Around that hole cluster the other structural gaps: the crack detector the kit wrote, documented and unit-tested (`test_find_tjunctions_sees_what_a_weld_audit_cannot`) has zero production call sites, and the weld audit runs in one builder out of nine, leaving every mint-heavy verb ungated for exactly the defect class the arc named as the seam carrier; the one bridge to a 3D DCC (`world-mesh-export`) reads pristine stock bundles only and has no `--mod-folder`, so an author cannot open their own deployed island in Blender, covers 2 of 8 mesh parts, and emits no material; the movement simulator that `coast_lint.py` calls "the oracle" is a study script while the 99-line static point probe is what ships; and 351 study scripts including 14 separate renderers sit with no promotion path, so each new defect class costs a new bespoke instrument. The through-line is that this project builds excellent instruments and then leaves them where they were born. My recommendations are therefore almost entirely promotion and wiring, not invention — every capability I am asking for already exists somewhere in this repository, tested, in a file that only one site can use. Sequence them: render first (it is the axis with a 0-of-13 predictive record and the only one where a picture substitutes for a relaunch), then wire the existing crack gates at the write seam (a small change closing a named repeat failure), then the deployed-bytes glTF inspection path, then the walk oracle. SUMMARY: The carry-vs-mint doctrine is architecturally sound and, at its core, genuinely composable — this is not an accretion of per-case verbs. `transplant.py` is a single coherent carry engine (one gather → rotate → shift → clip → gate → deploy pipeline, transplant.py:2715-2997) parameterized by a small mutation IR, and the 6,880 lines of `coastmorph.py` compile every cliff/beach/sand/cap verb down to just four of that IR's primitives (DropTris, EmitTris, VertexDisplace, SeaBump). `fuse.py` is a model of the doctrine done right: 223 lines that state the law (land cannot be knitted, water can), certify it row-by-row, and refuse. `coastscan.py`'s "the builders are the oracle — the scanner re-implements none of it" is exactly the discipline that prevents catalog drift, and `meshedit.py`'s construction-time refusals (walk-visibility, texel density, weld-exactness, winding) show the team knows how to encode a law at a call site rather than in a docstring. The `--in-place`/frame-safety and read/write disc-split reasoning is unusually careful for a solo-maintained codebase. The failures are not in the carry engine; they are in the three layers above and around it. First, composition itself is imperative and undurable: only `world-fuse` consumes a spec, 28 of 32 world verbs are flags-only, `island.landmass` overwrites deployed blocks with no guard while the interior verbs read those same bytes as input — and the project has already paid for this, losing Grimhorn's falls to a later island run with "no runnable script" for reconstruction, the same class of failure that cost twelve playtests on the Path D bench. Second, the knowledge that would make the next composition job cheap is stranded in markdown: three completed grammar studies (48 blocks, 62 components, ~10k instance pairs) produced fully encodable numeric envelopes — crest displacement ≤2.4u, segments never under 4.00u, turns 82% under 30°, fv=0 pinned at the crest, row-10 at 53% share and +0.12 v-phase — and not one of them appears anywhere in `world/*.py`. SUMMARY: The engine boundary here is, on the whole, unusually well-built for a solo-plus-agents project. The loose `.ff9mesh` override is a genuinely good architectural choice — one write seam (`mesh.deploy_override`), one format, one engine reader, identity-safe when absent — and it buys geometry iteration on Unity 5.2.3 assets that would otherwise require an AssetBundle repack. Three pieces are best-practice by any studio standard: (1) `discmirror.auto_mirror`'s EVIDENCE CONTRACT (`discmirror.py:28-45`, `92-200`) — it derives everything from the actual return values of this call's deploy writes, refuses to guess a game root, and explicitly refuses to mirror a synthetic namespace rather than relying on an accidental exception; (2) the s74 disc-role split (`s74:35-39`, `148-157`), which identified and named a silent-degrade trap (`assetDisc` vs `overrideDisc`) *before* it shipped; (3) `transplant.py:2936-2967`'s differential MISS gate, which maps every introduced miss back through the inverse tweak chain to ask whether the *donor itself* misses there — the "compare against the neighbour, not the marginal" law actually implemented rather than merely written down. The `placement.py` ground-query simulator is a byte-level RE of `w_nwpHit` and is the right idea. My concerns are not about the design; they are about a systematic asymmetry at the boundary itself. **Everything the kit validates, it validates against its own intent. Nothing in this pillar ever reads back what the runtime actually built, what it rejected, or what is currently on disk.** The engine-side ground-truth instrument for exactly this was written, shipped in s22, and has zero consumers anywhere in the repo. The runtime's rejection paths log to a file no tool in this project reads, and on reclaimed sea cells a rejected override does not fall back to ocean — it falls back to a *different real block's walkable geometry*, at the wrong coordinates, unlogged. And the deployed world tree (1246 live files, three disc namespaces) carries none of the build-provenance machinery the field pillar already has and uses. That combination is precisely what produced the two retrospectives this project already wrote about itself — "0 of 13 playtest verdicts predicted by a gate" and "twelve playtests sat on a silent regeneration revert." The fix is not more offline gates scoring intended bytes; it is closing the loop on actual bytes. Four of the seven findings below are small-effort and use facilities that already exist.
