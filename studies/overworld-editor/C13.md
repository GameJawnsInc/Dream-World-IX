# C13: a coast morph and a river move replay after an edit

**Status:** BUILT, 2026-10-24 (`c13_lab.py`, real bytes). Owner request: "Go on with read windows for the coast morph
and the river move, so they replay after an edit instead of always running again."

## What changed

C11 gave island, carry and sink steps a measured read window; a coast morph and a river move kept the whole world, so
after any edit before them they ran again (about fifty seconds each in C12's lab). `recipe.replay_window` now gives
both their measured window, and it is narrower than C11's: no ring.

| table | read window |
|---|---|
| coast morph | its own block |
| river (a carry or a move) | the blocks it changed: its new placement, and a move's old one |

A river step that recorded no cells keeps the whole world.

## How the windows were found: measured, then fenced

C11's recorder (every file opened, every existence check, listing and glob under the draft), on real runs, each on a
fresh draft of FF9CustomMap-world:

- **the morph**, `cliff-bump` 2 in block (5, 15), both its calls (disc 1, then disc 4 `--skip-mirror`): it read
  block (5, 15) on both discs and nothing else. Its neighbour (4, 16) is held in the folder and went unread. The
  in-place morph keeps the block's frame byte for byte (`transplant.morph_in_place`'s frame gate), so its neighbours
  weld to an unchanged edge: it has no reason to read them.
- **the river**, moved from 512, 192 to 512, 128: it read exactly the 15 blocks it changed (the 12 of each placement,
  9 shared), and none of the four held round them ((0, 9), (0, 16), (1, 16), (23, 9)). It composes from stock; the
  folder is read only at its own cells (ownership, the stack gate, the move's old files).

Neither run mirrors disc 4: a draft never does (Promote mirrors), so the draft's reads are what a replay must cover.

**THE FENCE** (`test_on_real_bytes_an_island_a_carry_a_sink_a_morph_and_a_river_read_only_their_window`, install-
gated): C11's four real runs plus a morph on the first coastal block no folder holds and the river moved to its first
free placement, and every block read must lie inside `replay_window`. A ringless window means something only where a
neighbour is held, so the fence warns when none is.

## The acceptance

**On real bytes** (`c13_lab.py build`: the real RecipeBar and the hill, coast and river cards, real runners, a draft
of FF9CustomMap-world): H a hill at (56, -440), then, clear of it, M `cliff-bump` 2 in block (5, 15) and R the river
moved from 512, 192 to 512, 128.
- H picked, opened with Edit, moved to (48, -440), accepted: **"The 2 steps after it are back on top (2 as they
  were, 0 composed again on the new ground)."** No `world-fuse` job ran. Accept and put-back took **7.0 s**;
  composing the three rows takes **48.9 s**.
- **I1: the edited draft == `world-fuse` of the edited recipe composed onto a fresh draft, over 1,739 world files.**

No game run: the replay restores the very bytes the cards accepted, and I1 holds them to the composed path.

**Hermetic** (`tests/test_worldedit.py`): each table's window, a river step with no cells keeping the whole world.

## Not here

Every table the editor writes now has a measured window; the whole-world fallback remains for a row no table names
and for a river step that recorded no cells.
