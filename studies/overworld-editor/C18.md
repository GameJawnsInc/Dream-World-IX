# C18: the game's fog in the 3D view

**Status:** BUILT, 2026-10-24. The roadmap's "fog in the 3D view, so the editor looks more like the game" ("Keep going
with the next roadmap item"). With "As the game sees it" on, the view now draws the game's world fog; the free view
is unchanged.

## What the game does, and why it is measured, not derived

Memoria's world map layers two fogs (`ff9.cs`, the weather update): the world shaders' own linear fog
(`_FogStartDistance` / `_FogEndDistance`) and Unity's GlobalFog image effect, with a height mist in the Mist years
(height 29, `MistDensity`), their distances from Memoria.ini `[Worldmap]`. The colour is the weather table's `fogDW`
(`StreamingAssets/Data/World/WeatherColors.csv`, which Memoria reads), and the camera clears the sky to it. The shaders
are compiled; their source is not in Memoria, so the equation cannot be read -- and Memoria's own formula, worked
through with this install's ini, puts the shader fog's end near 600u, which is not what the frames show.

**Mist follows the story, not the place** (`WorldConfiguration.UseMist`): on before the Exterior Continent (scenario
5990) and after disc 4 (11090), off between.

## The measure (`c18_fog_fit.py`, no game run)

C7's nine in-game stills (three spots, three turns), each one's decoded camera drawn by `render.raster` with its depth
buffer. The render has no fog and no lighting, so: near the eye (< 22u) the game's colour is the render's under the
game's light (a per-channel affine map, fitted robustly); the far land gives the haze; each pixel's fog fraction is how
far the game's colour has gone from the lit render toward it, binned by depth and by the point's height.

- **The colour is the weather table's daylight `fogDW`, (205, 217, 247), in all nine stills.**
- **Linear in VIEW DEPTH** (it fits better than radial distance: rmse 0.008 against 0.011 over 31 bins).
- **The Mist is a ground fog**: high ground stays clearer. Over depth x height cells, distance alone fits at 0.100,
  with a height term at 0.052: fraction = clamp((depth - 15) / (61 + 2.75 y - 15), 0, 1) -- low ground is full of fog
  by 61u, a peak 20u up only by 116u.
- **Out of sample, one spot left out at a time**: fitted on two, scored on the third, 0.051 / 0.051 / 0.066, against
  0.71-0.83 with no fog; the fitted numbers barely move (14-17 -> 62-63u + 2.5u per unit of height).
- **The years without mist** (C8's ten stills, scenarios 9500 and 10650): no fog inside 250u at this install's
  FogStartDistance 400 / FogEndDistance 490, and the Mist model on them is worse than none -- they are the other year,
  drawn with the ini's linear fog.

**On the view's own GPU path** (`c18_gpu_compare.py`: `worldview3d.render_still`, the shader the World tab runs, on
the real blocks at C7's poses): the far land's colour error to the game's frame falls from 54.7 / 93.8 / 91.8 with no
fog to **14.4 / 6.2 / 7.2** with it.

## What changed

- **`world/fog.py`**: the measured ramp (`Fog.factor` / `apply`), the install's daylight colour (`weather_rgb`), the
  two years (`from_game(game, mist=)`; the years without mist read the ini's FogStart/EndDistance), and `fog_still`,
  the software twin of the GPU's fog (a raster still and its depth). `Fog.note` says which year and, when the
  install's Mist settings differ from the measured ones (MistStartDistance 25, MistEndDistance 250, MistDensity 15,
  MistStartDistance_base 40), that the game's mist may reach farther or nearer.
- **`world/gamecam.py`**: `Settings` reads the fog's ini keys too (Memoria's documented vanilla defaults).
- **`world/render.py`**: `raster(..., want_depth=True)` returns its depth buffer.
- **`workspace/worldview3d.py`**: the shader fogs the scene by view depth and height in the game camera's mode (a
  missing texture's magenta too, as its twin does); `draw_solid` turns it off, so the ghost, the marks and the pin stay
  bright; the sky clears to the fog colour. A "Mist" switch beside the vehicle chooser picks the year; its tooltip says
  where the numbers come from.

## Tests

`tests/test_world_fog.py`: the ramp (and the highlands standing out of it), `fogDW` not `fogUP`, the measured colour
with no table, the two years and the note's ini names, the twin fogging by each pixel's ray (it first drew the sky at
60%: a ray up gave the sky a huge height -- fixed), the Mist switch. `tests/test_worldview3d.py` (native GPU): the
shader's fog against the twin on each perspective view, graded by the view's own depths (blob 0, differ < 1% within
two levels: float32 and float64 blends round a level apart), and the break-it: dropping the shader's height term goes
red.

## Not here

Weather and time of day (the evening, night and rain rows of the table), and the airship's altitude: the Mist was
measured on foot. The GlobalFog's own vertical gradient above the eye is folded into the fitted height term.
