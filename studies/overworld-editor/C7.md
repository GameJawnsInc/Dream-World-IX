# C7: the game's own camera -- how close the 3D view can come to it

**Status:** ANSWERED + BUILT, 2026-10-23; the corrected view owner-passed ("i looked, looks good"). The plan's open question ("how close a free GPU camera can come to the game's
own world camera"). Owner request: "go on with the open question ... (an 'as the game sees it' view from the 9001
rig's numbers)".

**The answer: exact in plan, and within the engine's own settle bands in height.** The free-roam chase camera is fully
determined by data in Memoria's source, the player's position and two runtime numbers the harness already publishes.
Nine in-game stills match a render of the decoded pose at a land-mask IoU of 0.873-0.995. A free yaw / field-of-view
nudge on top adds at most 0.006, so nothing systematic is left.

**And a defect found by asking: the kit drew the world as the game's mirror image.** `render.project` (and so the 3D
view, which A0 built to match it) took screen-right = fwd x Y, right-handed. Unity's `LookAt` frame is left-handed.
The stills chose the mirrored render 8 of 9 (one tie) until the fix, and the unmirrored one 9 of 9 (one tie) after it.

## Where the numbers come from (not the 9001 rig)

The 9001 rig is the SCRIPTED camera (`project-ff9-world-scene-rigs`: EYE/AIM actors), not the chase camera, and its
probe patch (s67) is no longer in the engine. The chase camera is `ff9.w_cameraUpdate` in Memoria's `ff9.cs` (the
version-matched clone at `C:\gd\FFIX\Memoria`). `world/gamecam.py` transcribes it:

- **`w_cameraPosstat`** (10 rows of pers, distance, height, correct and aim height, PSX fixed point; the constructor
  scales them by 1/256 and flips the heights' sign) and **`w_cameraElement[type_cam, place]`** (down / up / fly rows).
- **`w_cameraArea2Place`**: the cell's AREA (0..63) picks the place. Stock grass at the Landing Site is area 42
  (place 1), and the kit's lawn is area 14 (place 0), which have different posstats.
- **The blends**: down -> up by the player's altitude (`w_cameraGetHeightParam`), then -> fly by the tilt
  (`rsin(upperCounter / 4)`).
- **The pose**:
  - eye = player + yaw-rotated `distance` (`+(sin, cos)` of `rotation`) and `height` up;
  - aim = player + `aimHeight` up;
  - the eye is lifted to `cameraCorrect` above the highest of four terrain probes 5.5u round it, capped at 37.1u.
    On foot `cameraCorrect` (11.7u) outranks `cameraHeight` (~8.9u), so the eye rests at the ground + 11.7 even on
    flat lawn (measured: a 2.81u lift at the river);
  - then `LookAt(aim)` with the tweaker's Y nudges (558/256 on the eye, 312/256 on the aim).
- **The field of view** (vertical, as the kit's camera dict): `pers / 8 x FieldOfView / 44`.
- **The user's own Memoria.ini `[Worldmap]`** scales it all: FieldOfView, CameraDistance, CameraHeight, CameraAimHeight.
  This install has FieldOfView = 58, not the default 44, so reading the ini is not optional.
- **The runtime state**: `cam_rot` (yaw, degrees) and `cam_upper` (tilt, 0..4096), already in the harness's world
  block.

**THE ONE SLACK.** The eye's and the aim's HEIGHTS ease toward their targets and stop inside deadbands, 1u and 0.5u
(`w_cameraSetEyeAim`), so where they settle depends on where they came from. A pose rebuilt from position and yaw is
exact in x and z, and good to those bands in y.

## The measurement (`c7_lab.py`)

- `capture`: reach the world, then at three spots (the Landing Site on stock grass, C6's hill top, C5's moved river)
  three yaws each by a held bumper; settle 90 frames, read x/y/z, `cam_rot`, `cam_upper` and `ctl`, and take the
  engine's own still. Nothing is deployed. The game closed at the end.
- `compare` (`c7_compare.py`):
  - the decoded pose, drawn by `render.raster` at the still's aspect;
  - the land masks compared in the near field (the lower 60%, the party's sprite boxed out), because the game's fog
    eats the far land and render.py draws none;
  - each handedness tried, then the eye / aim heights searched inside their bands, then a free yaw / fov nudge.

## What changed

- **`world/gamecam.py`**: the decode above. `pose(player, rotation, settings=, area=, upper_counter=, ground=)` returns
  a `render` camera dict; `Settings.from_game` reads the ini.
- **The handedness fix**: `render.project` and `worldview3d.view_basis` take screen-right = Y x fwd (up = fwd x
  right). GL culls with the front face CW in perspective (the face toward the eye now winds clockwise on screen) and
  CCW in the top-down ortho stills, whose frame did not change. The orbit drag and the pan keep their feel (signs
  flipped with the frame). The GPU-vs-software parity and the bench cameras pass unchanged in the new frame.
  `tests/test_world_gamecam.py` fences it: a point to the camera's right lands right of centre, in the game's frame.
- **"As the game sees it"** on the 3D page's bar (`worldview3d.GameCamera`, the orbit camera's interface):
  - it stands the party at the last pick, on the scene's own ground (`ground_y`), in the cell's area (from the
    point facts), and frames it with the decoded pose and the user's ini;
  - a click stands the party elsewhere, and a sideways drag turns it, as the bumpers do; it neither zooms nor pans;
  - Home or the button ("Free view" while on) goes back to the orbit camera about the same point, looking the same way.

## Not here

- Vehicles: the decode covers on foot (`type_cam` 0); flight and the chocobo over forest take FieldOfView itself
  (noted in `gamecam.fov`), and their posstats are in the table but not measured.
- The tilt is decoded and blended but every still had `cam_upper` 0.
- The heights' settle state is not published; a harness key for `w_cameraWorldEye` / `w_cameraWorldAim` would close
  the last band (an engine patch: not worth one for a 1u band).
- Fog: the view shows land the game's fog hides past ~45u.
